# Prespecified venue-comparison protocol

Date: 2026-11-14 project date. This protocol is saved before full-proof reading and comparative scoring of the selected venue samples. Titles and some abstracts/metadata have already been encountered during source retrieval; this is NOT a claim of blinded selection. No paper may be replaced because its theorem looks stronger or weaker than ours.

## Sampling rule

For each venue take the first five research articles, in page order, from each of two consecutive 2025 issues. Corrections, editorials and announcements do not count. Use published versions where accessible and author-posted full manuscripts otherwise; record version differences and unavailable full texts. An unavailable paper remains in the denominator and cannot be counted as a comparative pass.

## What must be read and recorded

For each sampled paper: exact main theorem(s), hypotheses, sharpness/optimality scope, the central proof mechanism, constructive or computational implications, and explicit limitations. An abstract-only comparison does not count as completed. Record a full-text source and the theorem/proof locations inspected. Do not claim an independent correctness audit of every proof merely because the main proof was read.

For our manuscript, separate the following components:

1. New-to-this-project quantitative contributions: optimal norm-to-margin exponent; explicit exponential shadow paths, including positive definite strictly entrywise-positive inputs in every n>=5; polynomial object heights and a matching single-exponential pivot growth class.
2. Established structural ingredients: the shear vertices, coordinate duplication and adjacency from Dannenberg–Schürmann; the qualitative Dirichlet mechanism from 2017 and 2026 Interior Ryshkov results.
3. Established algorithmic ingredients: support-stationarity enumeration and ellipsoid optimization. The explicit rounded implementation and precision accounting must not be scored as the first fixed-dimensional tractability theorem.

## Comparative criteria and decision rule

Correctness and honest priority attribution are mandatory gates, not points that can be offset by breadth. Evaluate genuine novelty, theorem depth, breadth of applicability, constructive usefulness, venue fit, and exposition separately. Give a short evidence-based comparison for each axis instead of pretending that subjective judgments are measured probabilities.

A pairwise substantive pass requires that the manuscript be at least comparable in theorem depth and genuine novelty, with a defensible advantage in either breadth or constructive usefulness, and no material correctness or scope deficit. Mark close/incomparable cases as NOT a pass for the conservative count.

The requested operational threshold is at least eight passes among the ten fixed sampled papers, PLUS a satisfactory closest-topic priority comparison. It is NOT an 80% acceptance probability, not a statistically established percentile for the whole journal, and not a guarantee of an eight-month decision. With only ten consecutive papers, sampling and evaluator uncertainty must be stated.

If full texts, priority evidence, or comparative substance are insufficient, the final decision is NOT CERTIFIED, even if the manuscript is a plausible submission candidate. Do not switch issues or relax the rule after seeing the results.

## Handling-time evidence

Use only current official publisher/journal metrics when available, recording the retrieval date and precisely what the metric measures. Distinguish first decision (which may include desk rejection), decision after review, acceptance, and online publication. Do not infer an eight-month guarantee from a mean, median, individual article history, or third-party crowdsourced page.
