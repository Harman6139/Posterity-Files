## Evidence and scoring rule

For every scored paper record its exact main theorem, hypotheses, proof mechanism, sharpness, breadth of consequences, constructive content, limitations, and DOI; a title/abstract is insufficient. Read the complete central proof and necessary supporting lemmas. Compare the new manuscript along correctness, historical novelty, depth, reusability, genuinely broader applications, effective content and exposition. A mathematical pass requires all major proof gaps closed and an explicit distinction from the closest control. No points are assigned merely for general-looking notation, long proofs, computational tests or ambitious language.

A claim of outperforming at least 80 percent of this fixed cohort requires a substantive favorable comparison with at least 10 of 12 papers and no unresolved main-theorem correctness or priority objection. This small consecutive cohort is not a random statistical sample and cannot by itself establish a population percentile. The final report must retain that qualification. It is never an 80 percent editorial acceptance probability.

No Mathematical Programming or higher-venue percentile assessment will be certified without a separate ten-paper fixed cohort and full-proof comparison. Subject-matter suitability alone may be discussed without inventing such a certification.
