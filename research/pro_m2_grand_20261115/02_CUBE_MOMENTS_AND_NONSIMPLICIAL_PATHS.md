# Sharp bounded moments and nonsimplicial shadow obstruction

Checkpoint 002, 2026-11-15. Analytic proofs are internally complete; historical priority and independent checks remain separate requirements.

## Sharp bounded-moment correction

Let K_r={(t,z):t>=0, |z_i|<=t}. For integer q>=1 let G_q be generated by (1,y)(1,y)^T with y_i=-1+2k_i/q. Define Phi_epsilon to preserve all off-diagonals and A_00 and to replace A_ii by (1-epsilon)A_ii+epsilon A_00 for i>=1. Then Phi_epsilon(C(K_r)) is contained in G_q for 1/q<=epsilon<=1, and 1/q is the least nonnegative correction constant.

Proof: independently average q signs of mean y_i in each coordinate. Their variance is (1-y_i^2)/q, their means and cross products are preserved, and the resulting vector lies on the grid. This proves epsilon=1/q. Independent single signs prove epsilon=1, and convexity fills the interval. For sharpness set a=1-2/q and Q(t,z)=(t-z_1)(a t-z_1), nonnegative on every grid point. Its value on the corrected atom with z_1=y is (1-y)[a-y+epsilon(1+y)]. For every epsilon<1/q, a rational y<1 sufficiently close to 1 makes this negative.

Thus for rational A in C(K_r), D=diag(0,A_00-A_11,...,A_00-A_rr) is rational nonnegative diagonal and A+epsilon D has rational permitted Gram columns for every rational 0<epsilon<=1, preserving all mass, first moments and cross moments. Saturated second moments remain unchanged. A basic finite-grid representation uses at most d=(r+1)(r+2)/2 weighted rational atoms of height O(L+d(log d+log q)); four squares give at most 4d rational columns. Deterministic polynomial-time four-square computation is not assumed.

The dual map adds epsilon sum_(i>=1)Q_ii to Q_00 and multiplies Q_ii, i>=1, by 1-epsilon, preserving off-diagonals. Grid positivity therefore gives true K_r-copositivity of the corrected dual. A finite LP yields corrected primal factors or an uncorrected-target separator. Enumerating (q+1)^r grid points is not polynomial in binary accuracy. The binomial/Bernstein identity is classical; the exact constrained matrix statement is the novelty candidate.

## Nonsimplicial positive-definite exponential family

For m>=4, n=m+3, use coordinates (x_1,...,x_m,z_1,z_2,y) and K_m={x>=0,y>=0, |z_j|<=sum x}. Its 4m+1 extreme rays make it genuinely nonsimplicial. Set

 beta=25, h=25m+34, c=m(25m+43)/2,
 gamma=2h/c in (0,1), Z0=m(25m+37), t=3/5.

Define P_k[x,z,y]=(sum x)^2-(2k+1)(sum x)y+(k^2+k+1)y^2 for k>=1. Its minimal integer vectors in K_m are (e_i,z,0) with z in {-1,0,1}^2, and (u,z,1) with sum u in {k,k+1}, |z_j|<=sum u. This follows from the integer minimum of a^2-ay+y^2 with a=sum x-ky. Varying the boxes and the usual concentrated/two-coordinate compositions proves perfection. R_k=P_(k+1)-P_k has every x_i y entry -1 and yy entry 2k+2. The retained active normals have common annihilator span R_k: box variations force zz,xz,yz entries to vanish; axes fix the xx diagonal; concentrated and pair compositions fix all remaining entries. Both endpoints are feasible and the next composition violates beyond length one, so the exact edge length is one.

The full-minimal-vector anchor at P_1 is
 C0_xx=(25m+68)I+25J, C0_xy=h 1, C0_yy=c,
 C0_zz=Z0 I_2, with z cross entries zero.

For N>=256h^2 form B_N on (x,y) from F=I_5+(3/5)Adj(C_5) on coordinates 1,2,3,4,y, congruenced by diag(N,gamma,gamma,N,1); other x-coordinates have diagonal N^2 and zero cross entries. Set Bext=B_N direct-sum I_2 and A_N=Bext/(nN^2). These inputs are positive definite and entrywise nonnegative. The inverse-congruence Horn separator on the five-cycle is K_m-copositive, pairs -1 with Bext, and has norm at most 5/gamma^2. Therefore ||A_N||_F<=1 and dist(A_N,C(K_m))>=gamma^2/(5nN^2). The input/precision parameter is O_n(log N).

Put M_N=floor(sqrt N/(4h)). For 2<=s<=M_N set
 lambda_s=(sc-mh)/(2tN-s), C_s=C0+lambda_s Bext.
Its base means h_i=h+lambda_s tN for i=1,4 and h otherwise; top off-diagonals D_ij=beta except D_12=D_34=beta+lambda_s t gamma N and D_23=beta+lambda_s t gamma^2. The top diagonal is 25m+93+lambda_s N^2 outside J={2,3}, and 25m+93+lambda_s gamma^2 on J. We have
 lambda_s t gamma N=hs-gamma mh/2+(gamma/2)lambda_s s,
 sum h_i=s(c+lambda_s).

Let L={1,...,m}\J. Use positive composition atoms of sum s:
(i) for i in L,j in J, (s-1)e_i+e_j with weight D_ij/(s-1);
(ii) for pairs in L, both orientations, each with weight D_ij/[2(s-1)];
(iii) for J, p e_2+q e_3, p=floor(s/2),q=s-p, weight D_23/(pq).
If L_i is their mean, add s e_i with weight (h_i-L_i)/s. Large-coordinate remaining means are at least 59 plus a nonnegative term. For j in J,
 (s-1)(h-L_j)>=25(16m+34)/(25m+43)-lambda_s[(gamma/2)s+2t gamma^2].
The first term exceeds 16; using lambda_s<=cs/N and s<=sqrt N/(4h), the second is at most 1/(16h)+t gamma/sqrt N<1. All weights are positive. The last diagonal is automatic from the mean sum.

Fill the top diagonals with axes of weight chi_i=C_ii-s h_i+sum_(j!=i)D_ij. For j in J,
 chi_j=18(25m+34)/(25m+43)+lambda_s[(gamma/2)s+gamma^2(1+t)]>0.
For i=1,4 it is 2h-hs+lambda_s[N^2-stN+t gamma N], and otherwise in L it is 2h-hs+lambda_s N^2. The bracket is at least N^2/2 and lambda_s>=15ms/(4N), proving positivity under the N bound.

Lift each base atom with sum x=a (a=1 or s) to a full-support symmetric distribution of integer z in [-a,a]^2. Put T_s=1^T C_s,xx 1=2mh+lambda_s S_N, S_N=1^T B_N,xx 1>=2N^2, and v=(Z0+lambda_s)/T_s in (0,1). Each z-coordinate must have variance v a^2. An explicit distribution is: e=min(v,1-v)/2, h_a=(a+1)/(3a), alpha=(v-e h_a)/(1-e); mix e times the uniform distribution on [-a,a] with (1-e)[(1-alpha)delta_0+alpha(delta_-a+delta_a)/2]. It has the required variance and strictly positive rational masses. Use independent coordinates. This adds precisely the required z-diagonal and no cross terms.

The lifted positive normal decomposition has common annihilator exactly R_(s-1). Since <Bext,R_(s-1)>=-2(2tN-s)<0, this is the unique minimum-ratio shadow ray. Induction forces P_1 through P_(M_N). No vertex can separate because P_k is PSD and Bext positive definite. The ray is not copositive, witnessed by x=(s+1)e_i,z=0,y=1. Thus the unmodified rule makes at least M_N-1=Omega_n(sqrt N)=2^{Omega_n(H)} pivots in every n>=7.

The certificate uses O(m^2) base atom types and a variance formula, not enumeration of large boxes. This is a new nonsimplicial application of the prior orthant composition mechanism and known shear geometry, which must be credited. A generalized corrected-grid early-stop rule has not been defined or analyzed here; grid robustness is not claimed for this new domain.
