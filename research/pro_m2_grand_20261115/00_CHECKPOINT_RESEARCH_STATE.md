## Candidate master principle — UNDER AUDIT, not a novelty or flagship claim

Let P be a finite union of closed rational polyhedral cones and C(P)=cone{xx^T:x in P}. Let H be a rational affine subspace of symmetric matrices. A candidate arithmetic facial-reduction procedure repeatedly exposes a proper face containing C(P) intersect H, then replaces the zero-vector domain by the exact rational hull of its quadratic zero sets.

For an algebraic quadratic form W nonnegative on P, each zero x in the relative interior of a polyhedral face F has B_F^T W B_F positive semidefinite and (B_F^T W B_F)z=0, where x=B_F z and B_F is a rational basis of span(F). Expanding this algebraic matrix in a number-field basis turns rational zero vectors into the common kernel of finitely many rational matrices. The resulting domains are rational polyhedral cones. This replacement preserves all rational Gram completions and decreases the span dimension whenever the exposing functional is nonzero on the current matrix cone.

Prospective conclusion: in at most n(n+1)/2 reductions, either no rational Gram completion remains or the surviving slice is relatively strictly feasible. Its closure would then equal the closure of all rational Gram completions, yielding an effective criterion for measurement-preserving robust rationalization. The termination, algebraic sampling, rational interior construction and complexity claims must all be checked before promotion to PROVED.

## Candidate sharp application — UNDER AUDIT
