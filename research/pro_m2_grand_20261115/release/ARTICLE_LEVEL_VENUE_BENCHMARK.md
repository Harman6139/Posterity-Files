## Near-topic controls and substantive distinctions

The four principal controls are DSV's LAA 2017 rational interior paper, its Mathematical Programming 2021 simplex paper, Dannenberg–Schürmann's 2023 perfect-matrix paper, and Nishijima–Lourenço's LAA 2026 second-order-cone facial paper. The separate priority audit also includes Safey El Din–Zhi, Hillar, Scheiderer, Laplagne and Oertel–Schürmann.

The manuscript's strongest comparative feature is the exact rational affine-fiber theorem: a finite canonical rational polyhedral domain for viable factor columns, including a complete arithmetic obstruction rather than only a sufficient regularizer. Its exact moment-grid constant and interior local/global countertheorem add constructive and sharp consequences. These are structural distinctions, not numerical scores.

Against DSV 2017, the proposed theorem covers boundary fibers and measurements, rather than only interior factorability. Against DSV 2021 and Dannenberg–Schürmann, it determines rational completion without relying on local perfect-form discovery, while explicitly crediting their geometric ingredients. Against Nishijima–Lourenço, it provides arithmetic and algorithmic information in a different polyhedral-domain class; it does not subsume their second-order-cone face classification. These comparisons alone do not satisfy the twelve-paper gate.

## Venue conclusion

LAA remains a plausible subject-matter target because the main objects are constrained Gram representations and matrix-cone faces, with explicit matrix applications. That is an assessment of fit, not a verified percentile or editorial forecast. Mathematical Programming or a broader venue would require a separate registered comparison and stronger independent validation of priority and significance. No ten-paper higher-venue audit has been completed. No current handling-time or no-revision claim is made.

The requested upper-quintile requirement is an unfinished deliverable, not a mathematical theorem that can be inferred from a long manuscript or ambitious terminology.
