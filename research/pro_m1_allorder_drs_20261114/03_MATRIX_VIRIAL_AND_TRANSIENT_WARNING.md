# Checkpoint 03 — exact matrix virial identities and a transient warning

Date: 2026-11-14. Status: Sections 1–3 are exact proved identities. Section 4 records diagnostics and a conditional asymptotic obstruction, not a completed asymptotic analysis. The all-order last-step problem is still unresolved.

## 1. A matrix identity unavailable to general monotone resolvents

Let K=S_+^m, U=a+L, a=P_U(0), P=P_L, Q=I-P. For one plain DRS step write

    Z=a+x+y, x=PZ, y=QZ-a,
    d=TZ-Z,
    v=P_K(a+x-y)=a+x+d,
    s=P_K(-a-x+y)=y+d.

Then v,s are PSD and vs=sv=0. Equivalently, with R=2P_U Z-Z,

    TZ=(Z+|R|)/2, d=(|R|-Z)/2.

Since |R|^2=R^2, expanding the two squares gives the EXACT matrix identity

    (TZ)^2-Z^2=-(P_U Z)y-y(P_U Z)-d^2.               (1.1)

No commutativity of Z and R is being presumed: the mixed products cancel when the squares of their half-sum and half-difference are added.

## 2. Quadratic charges generated by congruence scalings

Let D be any real symmetric matrix and define the self-adjoint linear map

    B_D(W)=DW+WD.

Assume Da+aD=0. Put

    E_D(Z)=tr[D(Z-a)^2].

Then every DRS step satisfies

    E_D(TZ)-E_D(Z)
       =-tr(D d^2)-<B_D x,y>.                       (2.1)

Proof. Matrix complementarity gives <B_D v,s>=tr[D(vs+sv)]=0. Because B_D a=0 and B_D is self-adjoint,

    0=<B_D(x+d),y+d>
      =<B_D x,y>+<B_D(x+y),d>+<B_D d,d>.

But E_D(Z)=<Z-a,B_D(Z-a)>/2. Expanding its finite difference gives (2.1), since <d,B_D d>/2=tr(Dd^2). This proof uses only exact finite differences.

If B_D preserves L, self-adjointness makes it preserve L^perp, and <B_D x,y>=0. Therefore

    E_D(Z_n)+sum_(k<n) tr(D d_k^2)=E_D(Z_0).         (2.2)

This is a signed quadratic balance, not automatically a positive Lyapunov function. In particular one cannot infer boundedness or convergence of E_D without controlling the signed step sum.

## 3. Exact application to the doubled-arrow candidate

Use the order-seven affine space in Checkpoint 02. Let r_k be its affine coefficient on the direction

    Dn=E66+gamma(F02-F35),

so r_k=(P_U Z_k)66, and let y_k=QZ_k-a.

For

    D_plus=diag(1,-1,-3,1,-1,-3,-1),

B_(D_plus) preserves all six generators of L and annihilates a. Thus (2.2) is an exact identity for the candidate.

More importantly, put

    D_minus=diag(1,-1,-3,-1,1,3,0).

This also annihilates a. Its B-map preserves every displayed L-generator except Dn, and

    B_(D_minus)(Dn)
       =4gamma[(E11-F02/2)+(E44-F35/2)]
          -4gamma(E11+E44).

Consequently

    <B_(D_minus)x_k,y_k>
       =-4gamma r_k [(y_k)11+(y_k)44].

The exact charge recurrence is therefore

    E_minus(Z_(k+1))-E_minus(Z_k)
       =-tr(D_minus d_k^2)
          +4gamma r_k[(y_k)11+(y_k)44].              (3.1)

The last term is a feedback term omitted by a purely linear transverse-mode analysis. Its sign and accumulated size must be handled before declaring a persistent rotating mode. It is not legitimate to replace the nonlinear DRS map by its moving linearization for arbitrarily many iterations.

## 4. Numerical extension and what it does not prove

The diagnostic in Checkpoint 02 was extended to 2,000,000 steps, gamma=1. With the original rational perturbation epsilon=1/10, the scaled one-column transverse amplitude was about 0.02799524 and its scaled last-step norm about 0.01842491. At 200,000 steps they were about 0.02801495 and 0.01844571. The long-lived rotating mode is real numerically, but these nearly constant values alone do not establish its nonzero asymptotic limit.

At 2,000,000 steps, changing epsilon to 1/100 gives scaled amplitude about 0.00474896, still increasing on the late window. Changing epsilon to 1/2 gives about 0.01597274, decreasing on that window. This dependence makes a long transient a serious possibility.

An independent linearized calculation along the unperturbed three-by-three arrow orbit gives a principal-angle square approximately sqrt(3)/(4k), and linear amplitude damping approximately sqrt(3)/(8k). These constants are diagnostic/formal at this checkpoint, not a completed nonlinear orbit theorem.

The exact recurrence (3.1) exposes why a nonlinear proof is necessary. Formally, if a transverse state has amplitude of order k^(-1/4), then r_k is of order k^(-3/4) and (y_k)11+(y_k)44 is of order k^(-1/4), so the feedback contributes order 1/k to the charge. Its cumulative effect can be logarithmic. Treating that charge as a fixed parameter without proof would be an error.

### A rigorous conditional obstruction

Suppose, for a proposed candidate orbit, that

(a) sum_k ||d_k||_F^2 is finite;
(b) E_minus(Z_k) is bounded above;
(c) r_k[(y_k)11+(y_k)44]>=0 eventually.

Then summing (3.1) proves

    sum_k r_k[(y_k)11+(y_k)44] < infinity.            (4.1)

Indeed |tr(D_minus d_k^2)|<=||D_minus||_op||d_k||_F^2, so the signed-step sum is absolutely convergent. Under (a)–(c), a further lower bound by c/k on a set of indices of divergent harmonic weight is impossible.

Conditions (a)–(c) have NOT all been established for every candidate orbit. Thus (4.1) is not a proof that the candidate ultimately damps out, nor a proof of the universal conjecture. It is a precise test that any claimed persistent-mode proof must pass.

## 5. Current conclusion

The affine construction and exponent remain correct. The numerical experiment is not being promoted to an SDP counterexample. The new exact contribution is the matrix/congruence virial family (1.1)–(3.1). A successful continuation must either control its feedback well enough to prove genuine nonconvergence, or use an appropriate matrix-energy/face argument to prove universal saturation. No journal or priority gate has been passed.
