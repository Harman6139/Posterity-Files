# Milestone 03 — exact cone criterion and a no-orthonormal-basis obstruction

Date 2026-11-08. This result separates two different steps that the universal two-basis program must not conflate: (i) existence of a spanning positive rank-one POVM, and (ii) existence of an admissible orthonormal basis needed by the current signed-reflection certificate.

## Theorem 1 (exact Parseval rank-one compiler criterion)

Let W<=S^d be a real linear subspace containing I. Define
  Z_W={v in R^d: ||v||=1 and vv^T in W},
  C_W=cone{vv^T:v in Z_W}.
Then a finite family g_1,...,g_M of allowed vectors with
  sum_j g_j g_j^T=I,  span{g_j g_j^T}=W
exists if and only if
  span C_W=W and I belongs to the relative interior ri(C_W).

Proof. Necessity: the frame projectors are allowable and span W; their positive combination equals I. If I lay on a proper exposed face of C_W, every summand with positive coefficient would lie in that face, so their span could not equal W. Sufficiency: choose q=dimW linearly independent allowed projectors P_1,...,P_q. Since I lies in ri C_W, there is epsilon>0 such that I-epsilon sum_i P_i lies in C_W. Express that remainder as a finite conic combination of allowable rank-one projectors (conic Caratheodory gives <=q terms); then adding the reserved epsilon P_i produces a Parseval frame with M<=2q and full projector span. The cone C_W is closed: it is the cone over the compact set conv{vv^T:v in Z_W} contained in the trace-one hyperplane, so the relative interior and exposed-face arguments are valid. QED.

## Theorem 2 (Parseval does not imply an admissible orthonormal basis)

There exists a 4-dimensional rank-one-generated operator subspace W<=S^3 containing I strictly inside C_W and admitting a positive spanning Parseval frame, but NO two nonzero orthogonal vectors v,w can have vv^T,ww^T in W. In particular W admits no admissible orthonormal basis.

Take W={S in S^3:S_11=S_22=S_33}, of dimension 4. The only allowable projective rank-one vectors satisfy v_1^2=v_2^2=v_3^2, hence are the four lines generated by
  u_1=(1,1,1)/sqrt(3),
  u_2=(1,-1,-1)/sqrt(3),
  u_3=(-1,1,-1)/sqrt(3),
  u_4=(-1,-1,1)/sqrt(3).
Their projectors are linearly independent and span W: all have equal diagonal, and the three off-diagonal sign-character patterns plus the constant pattern have full rank. They satisfy
  sum_i u_i u_i^T=(4/3)I.
Thus g_i=sqrt(3/4)u_i form a positive Parseval frame of four vectors spanning W, and I is strictly interior to their simplicial cone C_W.

But |<u_i,u_j>|=1/3 for i!=j, so no two allowable lines are orthogonal. No admissible orthonormal basis exists, even though the frame is exact, tight, and spans the whole measurement space.

**Consequence.** A rank-one POVM compiler is not automatically a signed-reflection two-basis compiler. One cannot simply select d admissible rank-one vectors, orthogonalize by a congruence, and assert that the resulting identity remains in the relative interior of the transformed admissible cone. In this example every d=3-atom conic combination lies on the boundary of C_W; the identity requires all four rays.

## Theorem 3 (direct reflection certificate must use a maximal isotropic signature)

Let F in R^{d x N} satisfy FF^T=I, let P=F^T F, Q=I-P and R=P-Q. Suppose a diagonal D obeys
  FDF^T=0,  D+RDR=2QDQ>=gamma Q, gamma>0.
Then D has at least d negative eigenvalues and at least d positive eigenvalues. If D has more than d strictly negative diagonal entries, QDQ cannot be positive semidefinite: the negative coordinate subspace has dimension >d, so intersects V^perp (V=range P, dim d) nontrivially. Therefore D must have EXACTLY d negative diagonal entries, apart from possible zero weights, and the negative-coordinate columns of F must span V.

Proof. A d-dimensional totally D-isotropic subspace forces both positive and negative inertia of D to be at least d by Sylvester inertia/Witt index. If the negative coordinate subspace has dimension d+1, its intersection with V^perp has dimension at least (d+1)+(N-d)-N=1, giving a negative D-quadratic form on V^perp, contradicting QDQ>=0. QED.

This explains why duplicating every allowable Parseval atom and assigning opposite signs to each duplicate cannot yield the required exposing certificate: such a scheme typically uses >d negative coordinates.

## Architectural scope

This is not an impossibility theorem for all non-reflection two-basis dilations; a non-reflection design could admit a different stress or several exposure steps. It proves that the commonly proposed immediate Naimark/whitening shortcut is unjustified and identifies the precise missing requirement.

A successful unrestricted construction must (a) prove an admissible ONB after a controlled lift, (b) design a different positive two-basis stress without that ONB, or (c) change the source positive embedding architecture.
