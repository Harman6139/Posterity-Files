# Milestone 09 — generic lower bounds for SURJECTIVE two-basis SDP projections with bounded Kraus rank and auxiliary freedom

Date: 2026-11-08. This extends the preceding exact-positive-retract lower bound to many-to-one projections. The theorem is conditional on an explicitly bounded Kraus rank and/or auxiliary fibre dimension; unrestricted general positive decoders are not ruled out.

## Setup

Let m>=2, D_m=m(m+1)/2, s=floor(D_m/2), ell=D_m-s. Let L range over ell-dimensional subspaces of S^m containing I_m; choose A with range(A*)=L and datum b=A(I_m). The original bounded PSD fibre F_b includes I_m, is full-dimensional relative to I_m+ker A, and has affine dimension s. The real Grassmannian of such L has dimension
  g=(ell-1)s=Theta(m^4).

Let N be the ambient two-basis order. Let
  T={Y>=0:A_U(Y)=y}
be a nonempty PSD fibre, and let D:S^N -> S^m be a linear completely positive decoder with at most t Kraus operators:
  D(Y)=sum_{a=1}^t B_a Y B_a^T,
where each B_a is m-by-N. Assume SURJECTIVE exact feasibility:
  D(T)=F_b.
No positive/linear right-inverse encoder, no uniqueness of the auxiliary variables, and no fixed-rank restriction is assumed.

Let E be the minimal support of the PSD fibre T and d=dim E. Compress to E; its measurement span W<=S^d is generated by rank-one projectors and has dimension q<=2N-1. Let M:S^d->R^(2N) be the compressed measurement map, and let D_E denote the restriction of D to the minimal face.

Define the **auxiliary fibre dimension**
  a = dim(ker M intersect ker D_E)
    = dim T - dim F_b
    = D_d - q - s.
The equality holds because D_E maps the tangent space ker M onto ker A (proved below), and because T has a relative interior point positive definite on E.

## Theorem A — generic parameter/slack inequality

Fix integer bounds N_0,d_0,t_0,a_0. If the family of all such surjective representations with N<=N_0, d<=d_0, t<=t_0, a<=a_0 contains a nonempty Euclidean-open subset of the input measurement Grassmannian, then NECESSARILY
  g <= max_{d<=d_0, q<=2N_0-1, t<=t_0} [q(d-1)+t m d],
subject also to q=D_d-s-a, 0<=a<=a_0.
In particular every L outside a proper semialgebraic lower-dimensional subset fails to have any representation violating the corresponding strict inequality. Thus generic rational inputs satisfy the following tradeoff for fixed polynomial regime bounds:
  (D_d-s-a)(d-1)+tmd >= g,    D_d-s-a <=2N-1.       (1)

**Proof.**

1. Choose Y_0 in the relative interior of T. A PSD convex set has a unique minimal support E: take a convex average of finitely many feasible matrices of maximal rank, and every feasible matrix is supported on E. The compression Y_0|_E is positive definite. The image D(T)=F_b contains I_m, which is positive definite.

2. Show the exact tangent-surjection identity
    D_E(ker M)=ker A.
For H in ker M, Y_0+epsilon H is PSD and feasible for sufficiently small positive and negative epsilon, so D_E(H) belongs to ker A. Conversely, for each K in ker A, the matrices I+/-epsilon K belong to F_b for small epsilon. They have preimages Y_+,Y_- in T, supported on E. Subtracting their data equations yields (Y_+-Y_-)/(2epsilon) in ker M, and its decoder image is K. This proves equality.

3. Dualizing,
    L=(D_E(ker M))^perp
     ={H in S^m : D_E*(H) in W}.                         (2)
Here W=range(M*) is the span of compressed first/second basis projectors and is rank-one-generated. No exact injection is used.

4. Parameter count: a q-dimensional rank-one-generated W in S^d is spanned by q rank-one projectors v_jv_j^T, each specified by a point [v_j] in RP^(d-1). Such W form a semialgebraic family of dimension at most q(d-1). The t Kraus matrices B_a restricted to E require at most tmd real parameters. The map from (W,B_1,...,B_t) to L via (2) is semialgebraic on the locus where dim L=ell. Its image has dimension <=q(d-1)+tmd.

5. For each fixed m,N_0,d_0,t_0,a_0, the choices of integer d,q,t are finite. A finite union of semialgebraic images whose dimensions are <g cannot contain an open subset of the g-dimensional Grassmannian of L containing I. Hence inequality (1) is necessary for generic representability in the bounded regime. The complement contains rational measurement spaces because rational points are dense in rational affine Grassmannian charts. QED.

## Corollary B — quadratic ambient universality forces an enormous auxiliary fibre

Fix C>0. For a generic (dense open, full measure) family of rational input L with s=floor(D_m/2), any surjective two-basis representation satisfying
  N<=C m^2, t<=C m
must have
  d>=c_C m^2,
  a>=c'_C m^4
for all sufficiently large m. Here c_C,c'_C>0 are explicit constants depending only on C; for instance any values below 1/(1000C) and 1/(10^7 C^2), respectively, work after increasing the m threshold.

**Proof.** Since q<=2N-1<=2Cm^2 and tmd<=Cm^2d, the parameter bound gives
 g<=3Cm^2 d,
while g=(ell-1)s>=m^4/100 for all sufficiently large m. Thus d>=m^2/(300C). The auxiliary fibre dimension is a=D_d-q-s, hence
 a>=d^2/2-d/2-2Cm^2-s
   =Omega_C(m^4).
The stated conservative c_C,c'_C follow for sufficiently large m. QED.

This is the precise **slack explosion** needed to escape the earlier exact-bijection lower bound while keeping a low-Kraus-rank positive decoder: a quadratic ambient system can represent generic dense source data only if its exact PSD fibre has Omega(m^4) dimensions of decoder-invisible auxiliary freedom.

## Corollary C — superquadratic lower bound with bounded slack

Fix constants C,A>0. For generic rational L, any surjective two-basis representation with Kraus rank t<=Cm and auxiliary fibre dimension a<=A m^2 satisfies
  N=Omega_{C,A}(m^(8/3)).
More generally the tradeoff is
  g <= (2N-1 + tm) [sqrt(2(2N+s+a))+1],
which follows from (1) and q=D_d-s-a<=2N-1.

**Proof.** Since D_d=q+s+a<=2N+s+a, one has d<=sqrt(2(2N+s+a))+1. Substitute q<=2N-1 into (1). If t<=Cm and a<=Am^2, the right side is
 O_{C,A}((N+m^2) sqrt(N+m^2)).
To reach g=Theta(m^4), N=Omega(m^(8/3)). QED.

## Corollary D — near-maximal decoder Kraus rank is necessary on small compressed faces

If N=O(m^2), d=O(m), and generic L is represented surjectively as above, then
  t=Omega(m^2).
This follows because q(d-1)=O(m^3) while g=Omega(m^4) and tmd=O(t m^2). The general Kraus rank upper bound t<=md is O(m^2), so a solution in this regime would require essentially maximal Kraus complexity.

## Caveats and hostile audit

- The theorem DOES NOT forbid unrestricted quadratic-size many-to-one reductions. It exhibits a necessary tradeoff, not a universal impossibility theorem.
- Without an upper bound on decoder Kraus rank t, the family of decoder matrices can have O(m^4) free parameters and the parameter count need not obstruct d=O(m).
- The argument assumes a *globally completely positive* decoder; it does not classify all positive non-completely-positive maps. The theorem remains true for any decoder family whose real parameter dimension is at most tmd.
- The exact projection assumption D(T)=F_b is essential. A purely weak, tolerance-dependent reduction may fall outside it.
- The equality a=dim T-dim F_b holds at the affine tangent level because the source fibre contains a positive definite matrix and the lifted fibre has a relative interior PSD point on its minimal support.
- No choice of a right-inverse encoder is assumed; the proof handles genuinely many-to-one auxiliary fibres.
- Rank-one parametrization uses arbitrary real projectors after face compression, a superset of actual two-orthonormal-basis projectors, so it is a valid lower-bound relaxation.
- The generic family L contains I and its Grassmannian dimension is (ell-1)s, not ell*s.
- The conservative constants are not asymptotically optimal.
