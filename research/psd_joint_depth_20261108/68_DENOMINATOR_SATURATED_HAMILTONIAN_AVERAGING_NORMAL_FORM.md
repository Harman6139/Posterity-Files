# Milestone 68 — exact Hamiltonian averaging normal form at maximum dyadic denominator

**2026-11-08. PROVED combinatorially; arbitrary dense PSD necessity is conditional on the previously established sharp weighted-flag → mediated-network extraction theorem.** This is not the still-unproved exact depth-preserving dense-to-two-policy representation. It supplies a substantially smaller *complete normal form for the PRIMAL sharp weight data* at minimum target nullity.

## Theorem

Fix a uniquely solvable real PSD recovery problem with positive target rank, nullity \(\nu\ge2\), and sharp exponent \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
\operatorname{den}(2\alpha)=2^{\nu-1}
\]
in lowest terms. Let \(h=\nu-1\). Then EVERY strict harmonic midpoint certificate extracted from a sharp dense PSD arc has:

1. exactly \(h\) distinct internal levels, each of multiplicity **one**, and one terminal level of multiplicity one;
2. an **acyclic** full parent graph, with precisely one vertex \(X_j\) of reduced dyadic denominator \(2^j\) for every \(j=1,\ldots,h\);
3. a *Hamiltonian denominator-depth dependency chain* \(X_h\to X_{h-1}\to\cdots\to X_1\to\{0,1\}\), with \(w(X_1)=1/2\); for each \(j\ge2\), the two parent vertices of \(X_j\) are \(X_{j-1}\) and a distinct parent
\[
Y_j\in\{0,1,X_1,\ldots,X_{j-2}\};
\]
4. \(2\alpha=w(X_h)\), uniquely the weight with reduced denominator \(2^h\).

Conversely, ANY sequence of choices \(Y_j\) from that finite set, \(j=2,\ldots,h\), produces \(h\) distinct weights \(w(X_j)\in(0,1)\), with reduced denominators exactly \(2^j\), and its graph is acyclic and harmonic. The number of such **labeled denominator-depth parent circuits is exactly \(h!\)**, compared with \((h!)^2\) arbitrary ordered parent assignments. Their last weights are ALL odd dyadic numbers \(a/2^h\), \(1\le a<2^h\), attained at least once. Consequently every \(\alpha=a/2^{h+1}\) with a odd is an allowed exponent at target nullity \(h+1\), and its entire set of possible sharp harmonic graph shapes is generated by this factorial normal form, subject to the full-state barrier \(\alpha\le\min_j w(X_j)\).

## Proof

The integer midpoint matrix \(M\) has diagonal 2 and each internal row choosing two parents. By the determinant expansion into parent-choice functional digraphs,
\[
1\le\det M\le2^h.
\]
The reduced denominator of \(2\alpha\) divides \(\det M\), and the extracted flag has at most \(h\) internal levels. The assumed denominator \(2^h\) forces \(h\) levels, \(\det M=2^h\), and hence **every** parent-choice function is cycle-free. Therefore the union parent graph is acyclic. Moreover, its \(h\) internal null grades plus the one terminal grade use all \(\nu\) null coordinates, so all multiplicities equal one.

Give each dyadic rational weight \(w\) its reduced denominator-depth \(D(w)\), the smallest nonnegative integer \(j\) such that \(2^jw\) is integral. Boundary weights 0,1 have depth 0. If \(2w_u=w_a+w_b\) with dyadic parents, then
\[
D(w_u)\le1+\max(D(w_a),D(w_b)).
\]
Since \(2\alpha\) has depth \(h\), some internal vertex has depth \(h\). In a DAG with only h internal vertices, following a parent that achieves maximum depth constructs an internal directed path of length h to a boundary, using **all** h vertices. Along this path the internal depths must be exactly \(h,h-1,\ldots,1\), hence there is precisely one vertex \(X_j\) at each depth \(j\). The depth-one vertex's distinct parents are boundary 0,1, giving \(w(X_1)=1/2\).

For \(j\ge2\), at least one parent of \(X_j\) has depth \(j-1\), necessarily \(X_{j-1}\). The two parents must be distinct; the other has depth at most \(j-2\), and is thus \(0,1\), or \(X_k\) for some \(k\le j-2\). The only internal vertex with depth \(h\) is \(X_h\), so the distinguished doubled leading weight \(2\alpha\) must equal \(w(X_h)\).

Conversely start from \(w(X_1)=1/2\), and define
\[
w(X_j)=\frac{w(X_{j-1})+w(Y_j)}2,\qquad
Y_j\in\{0,1,X_1,\ldots,X_{j-2}\}.
\]
Because \(w(X_{j-1})\) has an odd numerator in denominator \(2^{j-1}\), while \(w(Y_j)\) has denominator dividing \(2^{j-2}\), their sum has an odd numerator over \(2^{j-1}\). Dividing by two produces an odd numerator over \(2^j\), so the reduced denominator-depth is **exactly j**. Hence all weights are distinct, interior to (0,1), and the graph is acyclic by construction. At each step \(j\ge2\), there are precisely \(j\) allowed second-parent choices (boundary 0,1 and \(j-2\) earlier internal vertices), producing \(2\cdot3\cdots h=h!\) labeled circuits.

Every odd \(a/2^h\) is generated: begin at the midpoint \(X_1=1/2\), and at each level take \(Y_j\) as whichever of boundary 0 or 1 is the endpoint of the dyadic interval containing the target \(a/2^h\). More precisely, use binary interval bisection with current endpoints always among previously created midpoints and boundaries; at step j the new midpoint of that interval has parents \(X_{j-1}\) and an earlier endpoint. After h levels it equals the odd target \(a/2^h\). This gives an actual harmonic graph. Its full-state leading exponent is \(\alpha=a/2^{h+1}\), provided the separate upper-barrier condition \(\alpha\le\min_jw(X_j)\) holds; for the bisection construction this barrier always holds because the target lies between the interval endpoints and every preceding midpoint is at least half the target. QED.

## Useful arithmetic recurrence

Write \(w(X_j)=A_j/2^j\), with every \(A_j\) odd and \(A_1=1\). For the second parent \(Y_j\),
\[
\boxed{ A_j=A_{j-1}+2^{j-1}w(Y_j). }
\]
The right added term is an **even integer**, since \(D(Y_j)\le j-2\). Thus \(A_j\) is odd. Enumerating the \(\le h!\) possible sequences gives an exact integer-only certificate catalogue for all sharp denominator-saturated dyadic weight systems.

## Scope

This result controls **the PRIMAL harmonic weight normal form** and is valid as a dense necessity statement precisely when the previously established dense weighted-flag theorem holds. It does **not** infer that the dense original kernel has the same facial peeling depth as its extracted primal harmonic graph, and thus does not solve the all-value joint dense combinatorial classification.
