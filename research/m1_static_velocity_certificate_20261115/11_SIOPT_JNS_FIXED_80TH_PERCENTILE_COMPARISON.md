## Editorial standard, publisher policies and methodology

**SIOPT:** https://epubs.siam.org/journal/siopt/editorial-policy . Requires substantially new results, broad optimization community interest and scholarly relation to prior literature. Suggested maximum 25 pages typeset in SIAM format; no supplementary proof files except when requested. Reviewed paper must contain its essential proof.

**JNS:** https://link.springer.com/journal/332 . Requires original advances that illuminate nonlinear phenomena beyond the technical area; introductions readable by a broad scientific audience. Current 9-day median first decision says nothing about likelihood or timing of final acceptance.

Fixed nonopportunistic sampling chosen before comparing contributions here: **first five OriginalPaper / original research articles in publisher display order** from each of two 2025–2026 issues:
- SIOPT vol. 36 issue 1 (2026): https://epubs.siam.org/toc/sjope8/36/1
- SIOPT vol. 35 issue 4 (2025): https://epubs.siam.org/toc/sjope8/35/4
- JNS vol. 36 issue 1 (February 2026): https://link.springer.com/journal/332/volumes-and-issues/36-1
- JNS vol. 35 issue 6 (December 2025): https://link.springer.com/journal/332/volumes-and-issues/35-6

Publisher display order differs from article numbering/chronological order. Do not select papers post hoc for favorable comparisons. Read official abstracts and accessible theorem statements/proof sections where open. SIOPT publisher full texts often paywalled; author arXiv/preprints were located for some but not all. Several JNS full texts paywalled; OA papers including the elasticity Gamma-limit and Navier–Stokes vorticity study were inspected into theorem/proof portions. **Therefore all relative judgments below are qualitative, not based on complete full-proof auditing of every comparator.**

Rubric inherited from the original handoff: Novelty N 25%, theorem/proof depth D 25%, mathematical generality G 20%, likely reuse R 15%, clarity C 15%; editorial audience fit is separate, and prior-work correctness/priority gates must pass before declaring upper-quintile status. No false decimal scores are assigned to inaccessible full proofs. "Lean M1" means an arguable *substance* edge, not a certified win; "unclear" is not counted as a win.

## Mathematical comparison summary

M1's most original core, if fully established:
1. A finite **input-data** convex SDP and dual gap certify the actual nonlinear DRS speed and all-start vector asymptotic on regular complementary support strata; not merely an abstract cosmic limit.
2. A non-Euclidean implicit norm selected by a *Euclidean* splitting algorithm: Frobenius vs Schatten 4 vs vector 4/3, including exact all-order examples and a bounded cross-block limit.
3. All-start singular-leading spectra for a large free-upper-block family with least-squares coefficient.
4. A universally asymptotically optimal moving local average of actual shadows for *all affine + closed convex semialgebraic set* pairs.
5. Separately, if the earlier long proof is independently confirmed, a rational 7-by-7 resonance in which natural-scale last steps have a circular cluster set.

The work is substantial, mathematically interesting, and more SIOPT-compatible now. But several qualifiers remain: the finite certificate needs support/facial reduction, the clean vector theorem assumes interior optimum, the singular case is not classified universally, the local averaging law does not give worst-case finite-iteration complexity or solve raw last-step behavior, and the proof chain spans different lengthy research notes. The most recently saved manuscript `spectral_transport.tex` **predates** the new certificate results; a coherent self-contained SIAM-formatted article with their full proof and complete related-work audit has not yet been made. SIOPT suggests 25 pages and normally disallows supplements.

### High-value actions for an actual top-quintile attempt

**SIOPT:** One additional *universally quantified singular-case/data-predictive theorem*, or a **nontrivial finite-complexity guarantee** for a certified output that surpasses existing ergodic/ADMM bounds on weakly infeasible SDP families, would substantially improve competitiveness. Then integrate the finite convex program, exact raw all-start coefficient, norm-selection examples and local recovery into a concise standalone SIAM-format paper with robust 2024–2026 references. Keep the spectral-resonance counterexample short if it cannot be proved concisely inside the journal page budget.

**JNS:** Prove a robust spectral-resonance theorem under nontrivial perturbations of the *operator*, not only starts and a special scalar parameter, and apply it to another nonlinear dynamical system outside affine–PSD feasibility. Then the paper could illuminate a general phenomenon at infinity, aligning with JNS's central mission. An algorithmic SDP certificate alone will not close that audience gap.
