# Research checkpoint — quadratic factorization beyond polyhedral cones

Date: 2026-11-15. Parent: ea809bed847d103a4c586428972d0aeda1b4d9d1. Prior results are unchanged.

The current extension is being proved and audited, not yet declared a historically new flagship theorem.

1. For a rational PSD matrix A and a fixed factor width s, the real Gram fiber {B:BB^T=A} has either no rational point or dense rational points. Given one rational point, orthogonal transitivity and rational Cayley approximation prove density, including rank deficiency. Thus every relatively open permitted subset of the fiber has a rational point whenever the unrestricted fiber does. Rational orthogonal density and signed Cayley charts are classical antecedents (Schmutz 2008; Liebeck–Osborn, also cited by Tang–Wang–Zhang 2025).

2. For a positive-scaling-invariant domain P relatively open in range(A), this gives the exact width law min rational permitted width = max(min real permitted width, min unrestricted rational Gram width). This holds for curved and nonconvex domains. The unrestricted rational width is at most 4 rank(A), by rational LDL and four squares. A quantitative signed Cayley chart gives polynomial bit height in log(1/clearance), with the base rational factor height declared. This is an existence/height statement, not an unqualified polynomial discovery algorithm for arbitrary open sets.

3. General homogeneous quadratic observations f(x) on rational semilinear conic domains (finite unions of relative interiors of rational polyhedral cones, allowing strict inequalities) admit exact arithmetic facial reduction even when their conic image is not closed or pointed. The rational viable domain is rational semilinear and canonical, rather than necessarily closed. Each algebraic nonnegative exposer restricts rational zeros through rational coefficient kernels. Reduction depth is at most the dimension of span f(P), or one less for a nonzero feasible target. This extends the earlier closed-domain carrier mechanism; proof and resource accounting are in development.

4. A sharper nonpolyhedral candidate has emerged. For the standard Lorentz cone L_n and J=diag(1,-1,...,-1), C(L_n)={A PSD: tr(JA)>=0}. For rational A with tr(JA)>0, all real columns can be chosen strictly timelike and the open-domain result gives rational factors. On tr(JA)=0, all columns are null and lie in rational V=range(A). Unless J|V=0, the restriction has signature (1,rank(A)-1). Rational factors exist precisely when this restricted quadratic form is isotropic over Q. A rational null point parametrizes a dense rational null quadric, and A is a relative-interior point of its Gram cone. This gives an all-dimensional arithmetic classification, including automatic rationality in rank at least five by Meyer's theorem. Explicit rank-three anisotropy candidate: V={(t,u,u,v,v,v)}, restriction t^2-2u^2-3v^2, and A=B diag(5,1,1) B^T; a mod-3 descent excludes nonzero rational null vectors. The Lorentz proof and historical comparison are under audit before promotion.

5. Boundary universality: two quadratic observations on a product of 2-by-2 PSD cones can encode arbitrary rational polynomial feasibility. Use F=sum determinant(block)+sum homogeneous linear-circuit-residual^2 and mass=t^2. F is nonnegative on the product cone, so an exact sum yielding (0,1) forces a rank-one block circuit in every contributing atom, and at least one atom has t>0. Divide by t to recover a rational circuit solution. Conversely any rational solution supplies one atom. This is an unconditional reduction, not an assertion about the current decidability status of Hilbert's tenth problem over Q. It will also show why simply replacing polyhedral vector domains by arbitrary spectrahedral ones is not justified.
