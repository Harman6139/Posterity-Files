# Single-quadratic arithmetic classification — proof checkpoint

The core arguments below have been checked internally. Historical novelty and independent verification remain separate gates.

Let q(x)=x^TQx with rational symmetric Q, and let P_q={q>=0}. The real Gram cone is exactly {A PSD:<Q,A>>=0}: rotate any real factor so that the diagonal of B^TQB is constant, using an induction on a Rayleigh vector with value tr(B^TQB)/width. The equality version is generated by null vectors. This real rank-one-generation statement is prior art (Sturm–Zhang 2003; Nishijima–Lourenco 2026 for Lorentz cones).

## Exact rational theorem
For rational nonzero PSD A, V=range A and sigma=<Q,A>:
* sigma<0: not real-feasible.
* sigma>0: rational permitted factors exist, with minimum width exactly the unrestricted rational Gram width of A.
* sigma=0 and q|V=0: the same is true.
* sigma=0 and q|V nonzero: it is indefinite. Rational permitted factors exist iff the nondegenerate quotient of q|V is isotropic over Q.

Necessity: every Gram column lies in V. At trace zero every column is null. If the nondegenerate quotient is anisotropic, all rational zeros lie in its proper radical, so cannot span V.

Sufficiency on the boundary: a rational nonradical null vector v gives rational w with q(w)=0 and B_q(v,w)=1. In hyperbolic coordinates q(av+bw+z)=2ab+q(z). The charts a!=0 and b!=0 rationally parametrize their null loci. A null point with a=b=0 is approached by adding tv. Thus rational null vectors are dense in the entire real null cone, including radical directions. The null-generated Gram cone is {X PSD on V:<Q,X>=0}; A is a relative-interior point. Choose a basis of rational null rank-one atoms, retain a small positive rational reserve on their sum, approximate a real factorization of the residual within the null cone, and repair the rational residual coefficients in the basis. Positivity of the reserve gives an exact rational representation. At most r(r+1)/2-1 weighted atoms, or four times as many rational Gram columns, suffice.

Strict case: all real columns can have positive q values. Once an unrestricted rational factor of a fixed width exists, its rational orthogonal orbit is dense in its real Gram fiber, so strict q positivity is preserved by rational approximation. Rational orthogonal density is classical, not claimed new.

Hasse–Minkowski translates the boundary test into local isotropy at every completion. Meyer implies automatic factorization when the nondegenerate quotient has dimension at least five.

## Lorentz specialization and a sharp counterexample
For Q=J=diag(1,-1,...,-1), changing column signs selects the positive nappe. A nonzero indefinite restriction to V is nondegenerate, because it contains a timelike vector whose orthogonal complement is negative definite. Hence every real-feasible rational A of rank >=5 has rational Lorentz factors.

The rank-four example in order five is
A=[[48,36,0,0,0],[36,27,0,0,0],[0,0,7,0,0],[0,0,0,7,0],[0,0,0,0,7]].
Its range has rational basis (4,3,0,0,0), e_2,e_3,e_4. The restricted form is 7t^2-u^2-v^2-w^2. A primitive integral null vector with odd t is impossible modulo eight; if t is even, all other coordinates are even modulo four. Thus no nonzero rational null vector exists and no rational factor of any width exists. Real factors exist by the real cone identity. Dividing A by 48 gives rational spherical moment data with a forced latitude z_1=3/4 and z_2^2+z_3^2+z_4^2=7/16, which has no rational point. This proves that the rank-five conclusion is sharp.

## Exact affine completion: canonical quadratic carrier
For a rational affine H, use the established rational-point algorithm for convex semialgebraic sets to compute the maximal rational range S of F=H intersect PSD intersect {<Q,X>>=0}. One rational witness X_* has range S: repeatedly find a rational point satisfying tr(Pi_{S-perp}X)>0 and average. This uses at most n+1 queries.

If a rational feasible point with positive trace exists, average it into X_* and return P_*=S intersect {q>=0}. Otherwise all rational feasible points have zero trace. If q|S=0, return S. If its nondegenerate quotient is isotropic, return S intersect {q=0}. If anisotropic, every rational permitted column lies in T=rad(q|S); compute maximal rational range S_0 of H intersect PSD(T), returning S_0 or EMPTY. No second isotropy test is needed.

Every rational vector in the returned domain is viable: subtract a small rational multiple of its square from the positive-definite-on-support witness, then apply the single-matrix theorem to the residual. Conversely every rational feasible column lies there. Rational points are dense in the returned domain. Hence it is exactly the closure of viable rational directions, independent of algorithmic choices. The closure of rational Gram completions is C(P_*) intersect H, and H meets ri C(P_*). Mixing with the rational reserve witness gives exact observation-preserving rationalization iff the target belongs to this terminal slice. Total query count <=2n+3, plus at most one rational isotropy decision.
