## 1. Primary comparisons actually inspected

### Bolte–Gaubert–Vigeral

J. Bolte, S. Gaubert and G. Vigeral, *Definable zero-sum stochastic games*, Mathematics of Operations Research 40 (2015), 171–191, DOI 10.1287/moor.2014.0666. Primary manuscript: https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.1967 ; theorem text: https://arxiv.org/html/1301.1967v2 .

Theorem 3 treats definable nonexpansive maps on a closed cone in finite dimension, for an arbitrary norm. It proves convergence of the linearly normalized iterates and, in polynomially bounded structures, a quantitative estimate for that normalization. The proof uses the tame discounted curve and its finite variation. This is a direct antecedent, not merely a vaguely related game-theory paper. The present first nonzero sublinear state term, natural-scale last-step obstruction, and static-excess/kinetic-energy identity are different statements; this comparison alone does not certify that no later paper established them.

Reading status: Theorem 3 and its discounted-curve argument inspected. The entire stochastic-game paper has not been independently audited. Discounting and tame one-variable arguments must be attributed as antecedent techniques.

### Liu–Ryu–Yin

Y. Liu, E. K. Ryu and W. Yin, *A New Use of Douglas–Rachford Splitting and ADMM for Identifying Infeasible, Unbounded, and Pathological Conic Programs*, Mathematical Programming 177 (2019), 225–253. Primary manuscript: https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02374 ; theorem text: https://arxiv.org/html/1706.02374v3 .

Theorem 2.3 identifies the unscaled displacement limit for firmly nonexpansive iteration and gives an epsilon-dependent estimate. The feasibility/weak-infeasibility results in Section 2 distinguish divergent states with vanishing steps from strong infeasibility. These results do not identify the proposed k^(1-p) vector limit when the minimal displacement is zero. A counterexample to that scaled limit is not a counterexample to their unscaled convergence. The set order in a splitting and the difference between cone-shadow residual and whole displacement must be retained.

Reading status: displacement theorem, weak-infeasibility statements and relevant proof portions inspected; not an independent audit of every algorithm and numerical section. The historical unscaled-displacement facts are not new contributions of this project.

### Do–Jentzen–Kuckuck, November 2026 revision

T. Do, A. Jentzen and B. Kuckuck, *On the gradient conjecture at infinity: Finite spherical length for gradient trajectories of definable potentials*, arXiv:2610.29211v2. Submitted 21 October 2026; version 2 revised 9 November 2026; 143 pages. Primary record: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29211 ; full HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29211v2 .

Theorem 1.1, proved in the later general theorem, asserts finite spherical length for every gradient trajectory of a definable C1 potential on an open Euclidean domain, in an arbitrary o-minimal expansion. It allows unbounded trajectories and does not require uniqueness of the flow. The paper also treats ambient metrics. Its continuous-gradient finite-angular-length conclusion is established prior work and must not be claimed here. A discrete conic DRS orbit is not automatically such a gradient trajectory; the circular last-step family and logarithmically infinite spherical length are therefore a contrast, not a refutation of that theorem.

Reading status: main statement, related-work discussion and proof organization inspected. The complete 143-page proof has NOT been fully audited in this continuation. The working manuscript must not say that it has.
