# Checkpoint 09 — two antipodal full-PSD-projection observations identify rational approximately feasible pencils

Date: 2026-11-15. Continuation from 7fc7758e0814e1c4b259b4ba3ab6b2e36c25ec9d.

## Status and exact model boundary

This is a proved recovery theorem for the STRONGER matrix-valued oracle

    O_X(q) = P_{S_+^m}(X_0 + sum_i q_i H_i).

It is not a proof or disproof of the original oracle that returns only the scalar natural distance and its coordinate gradient. In particular the latter oracle is not assumed to reveal the projected matrix or the hidden H_i. This checkpoint is saved as a separate information-model theorem, not substituted for the open minimax target.

The result covers every rational affine symmetric pencil with inf_q dist(X(q),S_+^m)=0, whether feasible or weakly infeasible. No isometry or escape assumption is needed for recovery. Thus it applies in particular to the normalized weakly infeasible class from the handoff.

## Theorem A — exact finite-class recovery

Let F be any KNOWN FINITE collection of pairwise distinct rational affine symmetric pencils of the same format (m,d), each with zero infimal distance to the PSD cone. There is a rational vector q such that the map

    X -> (P_+(X(q)), P_+(X(-q)))                         (1)

is injective on F. Consequently two nonadaptive antipodal matrix-projection queries identify every coefficient of the hidden pencil exactly.

The vector q and the finite decoder are effectively computable by rational enumeration and exact real-algebraic operations. Computation and query bit lengths are not bounded here. In particular, for known m,d and any known finite coefficient-height/description-length cap L, two such queries suffice uniformly over ALL promised rational pencils within that cap. The cap need not be tight.

Once the coefficients are recovered, any positive feasibility tolerance can be reached without further oracle calls: enumerate rational coordinates until the exact squared PSD distance is below the tolerance. Continuity, density of Q^d, and the zero-infimum promise guarantee termination. This is a query theorem, not a polynomial-runtime SDP algorithm.

The finite-class separation conclusion also holds mathematically for a finite collection of real affine pencils. Rationality supplies the finite bounded-encoding class and effective algebraic decoder; it is not needed for the underlying spectral identity.

## 1. Positive spectral visibility over a rational-function field

Write K for the field generated by the finitely many real coefficients under discussion (K=Q for the rational theorem), and let F0=K(x_1,...,x_d). Factor

    det(lambda I-X(x)) = product_j p_j(lambda,x)^{e_j}

into distinct monic irreducibles over F0. Each p_j has coefficients in K[x]: its roots are integral over K[x], so its coefficients are integral; K[x] is integrally closed. Since X is symmetric over a formally real field, its minimal polynomial is square-free. Let E_j be its rational primary spectral projector for p_j, obtained by polynomial interpolation/Chinese remaindering in X. The E_j are symmetric, pairwise orthogonal idempotents over F0 and sum to I.

Work in a real open neighborhood on which the maximal rank is constant, so P_+(X(x)) is analytic. A nonzero irreducible factor p_j is called positively visible on this neighborhood when at least one of its eigenvalue roots is positive there. The factor lambda, when present, is the persistent zero spectrum and is never visible.

Define the positive spectral module

    V_X = direct_sum_{j positively visible} range(E_j). (2)

Its dimension is sum e_j deg(p_j) over the visible factors. This is an algebraic/inertia invariant, not a definition in terms of optimal query count.

### Lemma 1 — a projected-matrix germ determines the pencil on its visible module

Let C=P_+(X) denote the matrix of algebraic analytic germs at such a neighborhood. If a matrix Y over F0 satisfies

    Y C = C^2,                                         (3)

then Y agrees with X on V_X.

Proof. In a splitting field L/F0 write E_lambda for the spectral projector of the distinct eigenvalue lambda. The selected branch is

    C = sum_{lambda selected positive} lambda E_lambda.

In particular X C=C^2. Every F0-automorphism sigma of L fixes X and Y; applying it to (3) yields Y sigma(C)=sigma(C)^2=X sigma(C). For each irreducible p_j, the Galois group is transitive on its distinct roots. If one root of p_j occurs positively in C, every root of p_j occurs with its full eigenspace in the range of some sigma(C). (Multiplicities are carried by the projectors.) The span of these ranges is exactly the sum in (2). Hence (Y-X) vanishes on V_X. No sign preservation under sigma is assumed or needed. QED.

The individual conjugates need not be PSD. They are used algebraically, not queried. Repeated eigenvalues pose no problem: the full eigenspace projector accompanies each distinct root.

### Lemma 2 — equal projection germs and complete visibility force equality

Suppose two symmetric rational-function matrices X,Y have identical positive-part germs at finitely many regular neighborhoods. Assume every nonzero irreducible factor of BOTH matrices is positively visible at at least one of these neighborhoods. Then X=Y.

Proof. Let D=X-Y. At each common germ C, both X C=C^2 and Y C=C^2 hold. Lemma 1 applied to X and then to Y shows that D annihilates the ranges of all their nonzero primary spectral projectors. Therefore D X=D Y=0 as rational matrix identities. It follows that D^2=D(X-Y)=0. Since D is symmetric over a formally real field, the diagonal entries of D^2 are sums of squares of its entries; all entries vanish. Alternatively evaluate at ordinary real regular points, where a real symmetric nilpotent matrix is zero, and then use rational identity. QED.

This argument handles persistent kernels without silently presuming invertibility. Using visibility of only X would leave an undetermined symmetric term on ker X; applying the argument to both candidates is essential.

## 2. Why two distant opposite neighborhoods give complete visibility

### Lemma 3 — antipodal visibility for a finite approximately feasible family

For any finite family in Theorem A there is v in Q^d and R>0 such that, on small neighborhoods of Rv and -Rv, every nonzero irreducible characteristic factor of every candidate is positively visible in at least one of the two neighborhoods. All candidates have their maximal rank on both neighborhoods.

Proof. A characteristic factor that is constant in x has real constant roots, since it divides the characteristic polynomial of a real symmetric matrix at every x. No such root can be negative: a constant negative eigenvalue would give a fixed positive lower bound on the distance to the cone. Thus every nonzero constant factor is positively visible everywhere. Only a constant zero factor may remain invisible, and it contributes zero to X.

For each nonconstant factor p_j, choose a nonconstant polynomial coefficient and a nonzero positive-degree homogeneous part of that coefficient. There are only finitely many chosen nonzero polynomials in v. Choose a rational v where none vanishes. Also require that p_j(0,tv) is not identically zero as a polynomial in t whenever p_j is not lambda; this is achieved by avoiding a further finite set of homogeneous polynomial zero sets. Such rational v exist by density, since a finite union of proper algebraic sets cannot cover R^d. Consequently every nonconstant p_j remains nonconstant after x=tv, and all nonzero-factor zero-eigenvalue events along this line are isolated.

Fix a candidate and put B=sum v_i H_i, A=X_0. By the eigenvalue perturbation bound, the eigenvalues of A+tB lie within ||A||_op of the eigenvalues of tB. Thus every root is O(1+|t|). For a monic factor p of degree r, this implies that the coefficient of lambda^{r-k} in p(lambda,tv) has degree at most k in t. Hence

    p_infty(z) = lim_{t -> +/- infinity} t^{-r}p(tz,tv)

exists and is the SAME monic polynomial for both signs of t. Its roots are real and are among the eigenvalues of B, with multiplicities.

If all roots of p_infty were zero, all roots of p(lambda,tv) divided by t would tend to zero. The spectral perturbation bound then places them, for all sufficiently large positive t, in the fixed bounded band [-||A||_op,||A||_op] corresponding to the zero eigenvalues of B. They cannot occupy a band around a nonzero t-eigenvalue of B. All polynomial coefficients of p(lambda,tv) would consequently be bounded on a half-line, hence constant in t. This contradicts the choice of v.

Therefore p_infty has a nonzero root mu. If mu>0, p has a positive eigenvalue near +infinity; if mu<0, it has a positive eigenvalue near -infinity, since lambda=t(mu+o(1)). A sufficiently large common R works for every factor of every candidate. Increase or slightly vary R to avoid the finitely many nonpersistent zero-eigenvalue events. Small regular neighborhoods preserve the required positive eigenvalues and maximal ranks. QED.

This is a spectral-factor argument. It does not assume that rational irreducible factors are constant matrix blocks, and it does not infer signs by applying a field embedding to an inequality.

## 3. Two antipodal point observations, not germ oracles

Choose a small connected ball B around Rv such that B and -B are regular for all candidates and have the visibility asserted in Lemma 3. For each candidate define the analytic matrix-valued map on B

    G_X(x)=(P_+(X(x)), P_+(X(-x))).                    (4)

If G_X and G_Y were identical on a nonempty open subset of B, analytic continuation on B would give equal projection germs in both antipodal neighborhoods. Lemma 2 would then imply X=Y.

Thus distinct candidates have nonidentical analytic maps (4). Their equality set has empty interior in B: at least one scalar matrix entry of their difference is a nonzero analytic function. The union of these finitely many closed, empty-interior equality sets still has empty interior. Its complement is a nonempty open subset containing a rational point q. At that q all tuples (1) are distinct. This proves Theorem A.

For effectivity with a known finite rational candidate list, simply enumerate rational q and compute the finite list of candidate tuples (1), using exact algebraic PSD projections. Stop as soon as the tuples are pairwise distinct. The proof guarantees termination. There is no need to ask the unknown oracle during this offline search. The two actual calls are then at q and -q, and a finite exact comparison identifies the unknown coefficients.

A known description-length cap gives a finite candidate list. The zero-distance promise is decidable by real quantifier elimination, for example through the projection relation C>=0, C-X>=0, C(C-X)=0 and the sentence that for every positive eta some x has tr((X-C)^2)<eta. Isometry, normalization and weak infeasibility may also be filtered if desired. This use of exact algebraic computation is only a termination statement.

## 4. Sharpness: one full projection does not always identify the pencil

There is an explicit four-element family of rational weakly infeasible isometric pencils of order six and two coordinates for which one projection query cannot identify the hidden member with uniform success greater than 3/4, while two identify it exactly.

For an interval [a,b], use four scalar diagonal entries

    (x-a)/2, (x-a)/2, (b-x)/2, (b-x)/2

and append W(z)=[[0,1],[1,z]]. Take the four intervals

    [-3/8,-2/8], [-3/8,-1/8], [1/8,3/8], [2/8,3/8].

The x direction has squared Frobenius norm four times 1/4=1 and the z direction is disjoint and unit. The scalar core is

    ((a-x)_+^2+(x-b)_+^2)/4.

The affine space is weakly infeasible, f(0,0)<=1/2+1/64<1, and the common beta=1/2 profile holds with the previous constants on 0<s<=1/8: choose any core zero with |x|<=3/8 and the standard W-tail point.

At every x>=-1/8 the first two pencils have exactly the same projected scalar matrices. At every x<=1/8 the last two do. These two ranges cover the real line; the tail is identical for all four. Thus every one-query location leaves at least one pair indistinguishable, and the maximal uniform-prior identification success is at most 3/4. This remains true for randomized queries by conditioning on their seed. The queries x=1 and x=-1 expose a and b respectively and identify all four.

This is sharpness for coefficient IDENTIFICATION. It is not a two-query lower bound for approximate optimization: the same-side pairs share a feasible endpoint, and optimization of this small example is easier. Identification and optimization remain distinguished.

## 5. A universal countable-prior consequence for the stronger oracle

Let pi be any fixed probability distribution on rational approximately feasible pencils of known common format, without a uniform height cap. For every delta>0 choose a finite subset of pi-mass at least 1-delta. Theorem A supplies a two-query decoder correct on that subset. Therefore

    sup_{two-query algorithms} Pr_pi{exact coefficient recovery}=1,

where the supremum need not be attained by one algorithm. In particular there is no fixed-prior positive-failure lower bound with more than two calls for this full-projection model. The same conclusion holds for approximate optimization under the zero-infimum promise.

This does not assert a two-query minimax algorithm uniformly over all unbounded-height inputs without supplied side information. It is the distributional statement required to exclude a hard prior for the stronger model. Finite-class tightness, countable-prior approximation, and worst-case unbounded-height complexity have different quantifiers.

## 6. What this says about the unresolved original problem

The original oracle supplies f and the coordinate vector <H_i,X-P_+(X)>, NOT the matrix C=P_+(X). Scalar responses do not give the matrix identity X C=C^2 used in Lemma 1. No method for reconstructing C from those compressed observations in a uniformly bounded number of calls is proved here.

Consequently this universal two-query theorem must NOT be transferred to the original oracle. The previously proved logarithmic lower bound in that model is consistent with it. The result does rigorously settle a natural stronger conic information model, with a concrete spectral visibility invariant and an exact explanation of why the complete projection matrix contains additional arithmetic information.

The target dimension-growing, unit-cost, exact rational value/coordinate-gradient frontier remains open in this work. The new theorem does not change its established logarithmic lower/power upper bracket.
