## New proof and reproducibility

Proof file: `37_SHARP_CUBICAL_CERTIFICATES_AND_RATIONAL_CP_REGULARIZATION.md`.
Proof commit: ff205766eb7a2075b4974cfa2952b2d826c597c7.
Code/results directory: `sharp_grid_certificates/`.

For n>=2, q>=1, let V consist of vectors in {0,...,q}^n having maximum coordinate q; let K_q be the cone generated by vv^T and D_q its dual. The exact least common coefficient in

    A+c tr(A)I in K_q for every CP A,
    Q+c tr(Q)I in COP for every Q in D_q

is c=(sqrt(1+q^-2)-1)/2. Independent unbiased coordinate rounding preserves off-diagonal products exactly, and its variance satisfies a sharp scalar square identity. A two-dimensional quadratic form proves the matching lower bound in every dimension.

Consequences proved:

- Every rational CP matrix has a rational nonnegative Gram factor after adding any positive rational epsilon I or epsilon Diag(A). The relative correction preserves every zero entry and zero row. At most 2n(n+1) columns suffice, with polynomial total certificate bit length. This does not imply polynomial-time discovery.
- A finite exact rational LP, at every grid level, returns either a rational CP decomposition of A+tr(A)/(4q^2)I or a true COP separator of A. Every nonmember is rejected at a sufficiently fine level. A shifted primal factor does not by itself certify original membership.
- A target-descent candidate R with <A,R><0 is processed by a finite objective-dependent grid into either a negative integer vector for R or a corrected strictly copositive separator of A. It need not be decided whether R itself lies on or outside the COP boundary.
- Strict COP margins and the necessary Ryshkov neighbors can be certified with finite rational operations and integer enumeration. The earlier shadow-pivot invariant and finite termination remain valid. The proof restates the finite-path argument, rather than assuming a black-box boundary COP oracle.

Exact tests completed: five sharpness cases, nine finite-LP alternatives, seven descent/certificate cases, and three strict-margin cases. The five-cycle example passes 2101 integer tests at q=4 and gives a strict separator with pairing -1/2 and cone margin 191/8320. These tests check implementations; the universal conclusions are algebraic proofs.
