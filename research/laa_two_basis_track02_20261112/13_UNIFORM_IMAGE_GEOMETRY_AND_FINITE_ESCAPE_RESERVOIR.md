# Milestone 13 — uniform geometry of every semidefinite image

Date: 2026-11-13. This strengthens the pointwise escape theorem in 12 to a theorem uniform over ALL bounded right-hand sides. Its main structural statement is that all unbounded lifting behavior is generated by at most twice the dimension of the lineality space of the closed image. A support-function identity then identifies the exact optimal lifting constant with a global error-bound constant and gives the sharp nonclosed-image exponent gap.

No trace-control, strict-feasibility, closed-image, or rational-coefficient assumption is imposed on A. Quantitative dimensional bounds use the proved pointwise theorem in 12. The elementary finite-reservoir and support-duality proofs do not use the two-basis compiler.

## 1. Definitions

Let A:S^m -> R^p be real linear. All data norms are Euclidean and all matrix norms in operator estimates are Frobenius unless marked op. Put

    C = A(S_+^m),  K = closure(C),
    D = K* = {y : A*y >= 0},
    W = K intersect (-K) = (span D)^perp,
    ell = dim W.

Thus W is the lineality space: the directions whose positive and negative rays both lie in the CLOSED image. This is not presumed to be the lineality space of the nonclosed image C.

For b in K and delta>0, define

    R_b(delta) = min{tr X : X>=0, ||AX-b||<=delta}.

The minimum is finite and attained by trace compactness. At delta=0 it is the minimum exact lift trace if b is feasible, and +infinity otherwise.

Define the uniform radius, capped image, and uniform approximation error by

    rho_A(delta) = sup_{b in K, ||b||<=1} R_b(delta),
    C_R = A({X>=0:tr X<=R}),
    H_A(R) = max_{b in K, ||b||<=1} dist(b,C_R).

Each C_R is compact. The definition of H_A uses distance to the capped image without additionally intersecting that image with the data unit ball.

## 2. The finite escape-reservoir theorem

Choose y0 in D such that S0=A*y0 has maximum possible rank among matrices A*y with y in D. Set E=range S0, F=ker S0. If S0!=0 set

    kappa0=||y0||/lambda_min(S0|E).

If S0=0 set E={0}, kappa0=0. Let w1,...,well be ANY orthonormal basis of W, and put

    P_A(delta)=max_{j=1,...,ell, sign=+/-1} R_(sign wj)(delta),
    L=max(1,sqrt(ell)*(1+||A|| kappa0)).

When ell=0, take P_A identically zero.

**Theorem A.** For every delta>0,

    P_A(delta) <= rho_A(delta)
                  <= kappa0+L P_A(delta/L).             (1)

More structurally, EVERY b in K has a decomposition

    b=A(B)+w,
    B>=0, range B subset E, tr B<=kappa0 ||b||,
    w in W, ||w||<= (1+||A||kappa0)||b||.               (2)

Thus one uniformly bounded positive lift handles the pointed part of the image. All possible divergence lies in the fixed linear space W and is controlled by its finitely many signed basis vectors.

### Proof

Every matrix A*y with y in D is supported on E. Otherwise its sum with S0 would be PSD of strictly larger rank, contradicting maximality. By linearity, A*y is supported on E for every y in span D.

It follows that for every symmetric X,

    A(X)-A(P_E X P_E) belongs W.                        (3)

Fix b in K and choose Xn>=0 with A(Xn)->b. If S0!=0, the PSD matrices Bn=P_E Xn P_E obey

    tr Bn <= <S0,Xn>/lambda_min(S0|E)
            = <y0,A(Xn)>/lambda_min(S0|E).

They therefore have a convergent subsequence, with PSD limit B supported on E and tr B<=kappa0||b||. Taking limits in (3) gives b-A(B) in W. If S0=0, every A*y for y in D is zero, and the same conclusion holds with B=0. The norm bound in (2) is immediate.

For ||b||<=1 write w=sum_j aj wj. Then sum_j |aj|<=L. Choose a minimum-trace positive lift of each sign(aj) wj with residual at most delta/L. Adding B to the nonnegative weighted sum of these lifts produces a PSD matrix with residual at most delta and trace at most kappa0+L P_A(delta/L). Taking the supremum proves the upper bound in (1). The lower bound follows because every sign wj is in K and has norm one. QED.

The theorem does not claim that finding y0 or W is a polynomial-time operation. Their existence and the inequalities are unconditional.

## 3. Closedness, a finite feasibility test, and bounded exact lifts

**Theorem B.** The following are equivalent:

(i) A(S_+^m) is closed.
(ii) Every one of the 2ell points +/-wj belongs to A(S_+^m).
(iii) There exists kappa<infinity such that every b in K has a PSD exact lift X with tr X<=kappa||b||.
(iv) rho_A(0)<infinity.
(v) H_A(R)=0 for all sufficiently large R.

If (ii) holds, one explicit admissible bound is

    kappa = kappa0+L max_(j,sign) R_(sign wj)(0).         (4)

For ell=0 this reduces to kappa0, so a PSD-cone image whose closure is pointed is automatically closed.

**Proof.** Closedness implies (ii). The construction in Theorem A with exact lifts proves (ii)=>(iii). Uniformly bounded lifts imply closedness: for b_n in C converging to b, the selected lifts have bounded trace, and a subsequence converges to a PSD preimage of b. The equivalences with (iv) and (v) follow directly from definitions and compactness of C_R. QED.

An equivalent face statement is

    C is closed  iff  A(S_+(F))=W.                     (5)

Indeed, every PSD X with AX in W has <S0,X>=0 and is supported on F. Conversely A(S_+(F)) is contained in W because every dual matrix is supported on E. Combining this with Theorem B proves (5).

This is a finite structural criterion, not a finite-time numerical decision test without conditioning. Closedness criteria and facial descriptions have classical antecedents; no claim that qualitative closedness was previously open is made.

## 4. Exact duality with the homogeneous matrix-inequality error bound

For y in R^p put

    nu(y)=max(0,-lambda_min(A*y)),
    psi(y)=dist(y,D),
    h_A(epsilon)=max{psi(y):||y||<=1, nu(y)<=epsilon}.

The maximum in h_A is attained. It measures distance to the HOMOGENEOUS dual LMI solution cone, not distance to an arbitrary nonzero primal feasible fibre.

**Theorem C.** For every R>=0,

    H_A(R)=max_(||y||<=1) [psi(y)-R nu(y)]              (6)
          =max_(0<=epsilon<=nu_max)
                    [h_A(epsilon)-R epsilon],          (7)

where nu_max=max_{||y||<=1}nu(y). Also

    H_A(R)<=delta  iff  rho_A(delta)<=R.                (8)

Consequently, with extended-real values allowed,

    rho_A(0)
       = inf{kappa>=0:dist(y,D)<=kappa nu(y) for all y}.
                                                               (9)

In particular, the image is closed IF AND ONLY IF the homogeneous dual matrix inequality has a global Lipschitz error bound. The best trace-lifting constant and the best error-bound constant are EXACTLY equal in the stated norms.

### Proof

Distance to the compact convex set C_R has the support representation

    dist(b,C_R)=max_{||u||<=1} [<u,b>-support(C_R,u)].

Taking the maximum over b in K intersect the unit ball and interchanging two maxima gives

    H_A(R)=max_{||u||<=1}
       [support(K intersect unit ball,u)-support(C_R,u)].

For a closed convex cone K, Moreau's orthogonal decomposition gives

    support(K intersect unit ball,u)=dist(u,K^polar).

Also

    support(C_R,u)=R max(0,lambda_max(A*u)).

Since K^polar=-D, substitution u=-y proves (6). To prove (7), for any y use epsilon=nu(y); conversely an optimizer defining h_A(epsilon) has nu(y)<=epsilon, so h_A(epsilon)-R epsilon<=psi(y)-R nu(y)<=H_A(R). This yields equality. No concavity of h_A is asserted or needed.

The minima defining R_b and distances to C_R are attained, so (8) follows pointwise and then uniformly. Finally H_A(kappa)=0 iff psi(y)<=kappa nu(y) on the unit ball, and homogeneity extends the inequality to all y. Apply Theorem B. QED.

If an actual Hausdorff metric on bounded sets is desired, let

    J_A(R)=d_H(K intersect unit ball, C_R intersect unit ball).

Because C_R is contained in K and radial contraction preserves C_R,

    H_A(R)<=J_A(R)<=2H_A(R).                            (10)

The upper inequality follows by radially contracting a nearest point z in C_R whenever ||z||>1: its contraction moves it by at most ||z-b|| for ||b||<=1. Thus H_A determines the exact Hausdorff power exponent as well.

## 5. A stronger obstruction: nonclosedness always has a reciprocal-linear witness

The pointwise theorem in 12 allows escape exponents below one for particular data. Uniformly over all data of one nonclosed map, that cannot be the worst exponent.

**Theorem D.** If C is not closed, there is a UNIT vector w in W\C and constants c,delta0>0 such that

    R_w(delta)>=c/delta       (0<delta<delta0).          (11)

Thus some weakly infeasible datum in the lineality space always requires at least reciprocal-linear trace growth.

### Proof

By (5), C_F=A(S_+(F)) is a proper convex cone in W. A proper convex cone in a finite-dimensional vector space cannot be dense in that entire space: the relative interior of a convex set agrees with that of its closure, or equivalently one can approximate the vertices of a simplex surrounding an arbitrary point. Therefore its closure is proper and has a nonzero separating functional. Choose y in W, ||y||=1, with

    <y,A(Z)>=0 for every Z>=0 supported on F.

Writing V=A*y in E/F blocks, this means V_FF>=0. Set w=-y. Then w is in W, hence in K, but it has no PSD exact preimage: any such preimage would be supported on F and would have <y,AX>>=0, whereas <y,w>=-1. Thus w is weakly infeasible.

Nonclosedness forces S0!=0. Otherwise D=ker A*, so K=range A is a vector space; the convex cone C would be dense in its entire span and hence equal to it.

For a PSD X with ||AX-w||<=delta, the equality <y0,w>=0 gives

    tr X_EE<=kappa0 delta.

Also <y,AX><=-1+delta. Positivity of the FF block of V and PSD block Cauchy-Schwarz give

    <y,AX>
      >=-||V_EE||_op kappa0 delta
         -2||V_EF||_F sqrt(kappa0 delta tr X).

The cross block V_EF must be nonzero: otherwise sufficiently accurate approximate preimages of w could not exist. For delta<=1/2 and ||V_EE||_op kappa0 delta<=1/4, the last display implies

    tr X >= 1/[64 ||V_EF||_F^2 kappa0 delta].

This proves (11). QED.

The witness need not be computable from floating-point information alone. The theorem is exact and does not assert a polynomial lower bound on the displayed constant.

## 6. Sharp uniform exponent dichotomy

Put E_m=2^(m-1)-1 for m>=2.

**Theorem E.** Exactly one of the following occurs.

### Closed-image case

There is a finite exact lifting radius rho_A(0), and H_A(R)=0 once R>=rho_A(0). The dual homogeneous LMI has a global Lipschitz error bound with precisely that best constant. If A!=0, the optimal local power of h_A is one. If A=0, both errors vanish identically.

### Nonclosed-image case

There are positive constants P,h0,D0 and rational numbers a,beta,theta such that

    rho_A(delta) ~ P delta^(-a),
    H_A(R) ~ D0 R^(-beta),
    h_A(epsilon) ~ h0 epsilon^theta,

with

    1<=a<=E_m,
    beta=1/a in [1/E_m,1],
    theta=1/(a+1) in [2^(-(m-1)),1/2].                (12)

In particular there is no non-Lipschitz homogeneous dual error exponent strictly between 1/2 and 1. The exact leading constants satisfy

    D0=(1-theta) theta^(theta/(1-theta))
                         h0^(1/(1-theta)),
    P=theta (1-theta)^a h0^(a+1).                     (13)

Moreover the uniform exponent a is the largest POINTWISE escape exponent among the finitely many weakly infeasible signed basis data +/-wj. Consequently a single fixed datum in any such signed basis realizes the uniform worst exponent.

### Proof

Every signed basis datum is either feasible or weakly infeasible. For feasible data its trace profile stays bounded at zero. For weakly infeasible data 12 supplies a positive rational exponent at most E_m. The finite comparison (1) gives uniform finiteness, rho_A(delta)=O(delta^(-E_m)), and equality of the uniform power exponent with the largest one among the signed basis data. Theorem D gives rho_A(delta)>=c/delta, so a>=1.

All the value functions are semialgebraic by finite-dimensional real quantifier elimination. This includes the uniform supremum: its domain K intersect the unit ball is semialgebraic, and (1) has already proved the supremum finite for positive delta. Thus positive leading powers and coefficients exist. Equation (8) makes beta=1/a.

For h_A, compactness gives h_A(epsilon)->0. If its exponent were one, h_A(epsilon)<=constant times epsilon near zero, and compactness away from zero plus homogeneity would give a global Lipschitz error bound. The image would then be closed by (9). Also its exponent cannot exceed one when A!=0: scaling any point outside D gives a linear lower bound. Therefore in the nonclosed case 0<theta<1.

By (7), large R only allows epsilon tending to zero to contribute a positive value. Substituting the asymptotic h_A(epsilon)~h0 epsilon^theta and maximizing h0 epsilon^theta-R epsilon gives

    H_A(R) ~ (1-theta) theta^(theta/(1-theta))
                    h0^(1/(1-theta)) R^(-theta/(1-theta)).

The relative asymptotic error is uniform on sufficiently small epsilon intervals, so this maximization is justified by upper and lower multiplicative bounds on h_A. It gives beta=theta/(1-theta), hence theta=1/(a+1). Inverting H_A gives (13). This proves the complete dichotomy. QED.

Equivalently every nonclosed image has, for large R,

    c/R <= H_A(R) <= C R^(-1/E_m),                    (14)

with map-dependent positive constants. Both endpoint exponents are optimal at every matrix order m>=2.

### Sharpness at a=1

Use A(X)=(X_00,X_01) on a 2-by-2 principal block, ignoring any additional coordinates. Its closed image is the half-plane {(s,t):s>=0}. For ||(s,t)||<=1 and 0<delta<=1,

    X=[[s+delta,t],[t,t^2/(s+delta)]] >=0

has residual delta and trace <=1+delta+1/delta. Thus rho_A(delta)=O(1/delta). The datum (0,1) gives the matching lower bound. Hence a=1, beta=1, theta=1/2, at every m by zero padding.

### Sharpness at a=E_m

Use the rational map from 12 with data equations

    X_00=0, X_01=1,
    X_ii-X_(0,i+1)=0 for 1<=i<=m-2.

The unit datum (0,1,0,...,0) has trace profile Theta(delta^(-E_m)), by the explicit repeated-squaring lower bound and rank-one curve in 12. The uniform upper bound just proved gives a=E_m. Therefore beta=1/E_m and theta=2^(-(m-1)). No statement that all exponents between the endpoints occur at fixed m is made.

## 7. Scope and relationship to prior work

The new continuation proves uniform quantitative statements, a finite-witness principle, an exact norm-constant identity, and sharp exponent relations. The qualitative study of closed semidefinite images is established, including G. Pataki, On the Closedness of the Linear Image of a Closed Convex Cone (Mathematics of Operations Research, 2007), and M. Liu and G. Pataki, Exact duals and short certificates of infeasibility and weak infeasibility in conic linear programming (2017). Their existence is not being overwritten by a claim that closedness was an open problem.
